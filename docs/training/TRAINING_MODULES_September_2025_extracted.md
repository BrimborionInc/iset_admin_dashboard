## Slide 3: Contribution Agreement
- Contribution Agreement
- In April 2019, NWAC signed a ten-year Contribution Agreement with Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) to deliver the Indigenous Skills and Employment Training (ISET) program. This program and the current funding are scheduled to end on March 31, 2029.
- The Contribution Agreement  that NWAC holds with ESDC is valued at approximately $33,000,000 over the term of the Agreement.
- There are two annual funding streams within the Contribution Agreement:
  - Employment Insurance (EI) – for those clients eligible to be funded under the Employment Insurance Program.
  - Consolidated Revenue Fund (CRF) – for those clients or expenses that are not eligible to be funded under EI.
  - ESDC sets the amounts eligible under the EI Stream.
  - Funds from the CRF stream can be reallocated to the EI stream but the reverse is not permissible.

## Slide 4: NWAC & PTMA
- NWAC & PTMA
- NWAC - Native Women’s Association of Canada
- Established in 1974 (50th year in 2024)
- We were founded on the collective goal to enhance, promote, and foster the social, economic, cultural, and political well-being of Indigenous women and girls within their respective communities and Canadian societies. NWAC also defends the rights, delivers programming to, and amplifies the perspectives of Indigenous women in their diversities.
- NWAC is inclusive of First Nations—on- and off-reserve, status, non-status, and disenfranchised—Inuit, and Métis.

## Slide 5: Background
- Background
- ISET Holders are Indigenous organizations that have the designated authority through Contribution Agreements with ESDC to act on behalf of Canada in the delivery of the ISET program.
- There are approximately 115 regional ISET holders across Canada. The Native Women’s Association of Canada (NWAC) is one of two national ISET Holders.
- The Native Women’s Association of Canada (NWAC) provides employment and training support services across Canada to Indigenous women and girls in all their diversities. The National Office provides overall coordination, administration and auditing of its program in all provinces and territories to ensure compliance with all aspects of the program.
- Where applicable, and through sub-agreements, NWAC works in collaboration with our Provincial-Territorial Member Associations (PTMAs), to deliver the program regionally/territorially.

## Slide 6: Mandate
- Mandate
- The Mandate of the ISETs program is to increase Indigenous participation in the Canadian labour market. The program supports First Nations, Metis and Inuit persons with access to sustainable and meaningful employment by closing the employment and earnings gaps between Indigenous and non-Indigenous people by placing greater emphasis on training and education.
- The ISET program provides access to training and employment supports to Canadian Indigenous women in their diversities, including status and non-status First Nations, Metis and Inuit peoples whether residing on or off-reserve, in urban centres and in rural, remote communities.
- The ISET program is intended to provide financial supports such as tuition, living allowance, childcare, job starts and other resources to help eligible Indigenous women in all their diversities to reach their employment and skills training goals.

## Slide 7: Eligibility
- Eligibility
- Canadian citizen
- Indigenous women in all their diversities
  - status and non-status First Nations, Metis and Inuit peoples whether residing on or off-reserve, in urban centres and in rural, remote communities
- Be unemployed, under-employed, or can demonstrate an immediate risk of becoming unemployed.
- Active NWAC or PTMA staff (including Board members) are NOT eligible to receive funding under the ISET program or by its partner organizations.

## Slide 12: InterventionResearch
- InterventionResearch
    - As the Case Manager, it is your role to conduct appropriate research or assessment of the client’s career/educational or training choice which includes but is not limited to:
  - Developing informed and realistic career plans and guiding the client towards appropriate resources;
  - Assisting the client to understand the physical and/or mental challenges associated with different occupations;
  - Considering career paths that are emerging or are in high demand and discussing the wage scale and ability to progress within the occupation (technology, AI, red seal trades, etc.);
  - Ensuring the client obtains the necessary skills required (competencies) and credentials to help increase their participation in the Canadian labour force;
  - Verifying that the program leads to viable employment opportunities in a related field;
  - Has the potential to help close the earnings gap between Indigenous and non-Indigenous  peoples.

## Slide 17: Funding Stream(ITP)
- Funding Stream(ITP)
- The Individual Training Purchase
- Provides clients with the necessary skills and qualifications that are needed to enter and perform within a chosen occupation by enrolling them in the appropriate training or educational program
- Provides financial assistance to eligible clients to help pay for the cost of  education, skills training courses, related program expenses and living expenses.
- Searchable database of over 10,000 academic programs offered leading to an undergraduate or graduate degree from a recognized degree-granting institution: http://oraweb.aucc.ca/dcu_e.html.

## Slide 18: Funding Stream(TWS)
- Funding Stream(TWS)
- Targeted Wage Subsidy
- A financial assistance program which encourages employers to hire individuals for a specific length of time, with the objective of providing the client with on-the-job training and sufficient skills to obtain employment.
- Employers are considered as program partners and must focus on providing appropriate orientation and training  that support employment, training and goals of the client. Non-profit organizations are eligible for maximum subsidy. Private sector employers are eligible for 50% of wages, MERCs and vacation pay.
- Employer must provide a detailed employment plan, including a defined orientation period and job description.
- Student Employment  - intended to help provide employment to students with the opportunity to develop and enhance employability and occupational skills with practical on-the-job experience. Student  must be enrolled in full-time studies.

## Slide 19: Targeted Wage Subsidy
- Targeted Wage Subsidy
- Required Documents
- Employer:
- 1) Letter stating what employment duties would be;
- 2) Letter of offer after the Targeted Wage Subsidy is over;
- Case Managers cannot move forward with a Targeted Wage Subsidy application without these documents from the employer.
- The base amount per hour depends on the Province/Territory but cannot be less than the minimum wage in that specific region.
- Subsidy can support up to 8 hours per day.
- MERCS – The employer determines the MERCS (this amount is dependent on the amount of money you are recommending to pay per hour).

## Slide 20: Funding Streams(JCP)
- Funding Streams(JCP)
- The Job Creation Partnership (JCP)
- Provides financial assistance to enable unemployed persons to gain work experience by creating temporary jobs.
- Project activities should benefit both the participant client and the community
- Partnerships with public sector and non-profit employers are allowed – NO private companies
- Aim is to improve the client’s long-term employment prospects

## Slide 21: Funding Streams(SEB)
- Funding Streams(SEB)
- The Self-Employment Benefits
- Program provides direct financial assistance, business training or professional support to enable a client to start their own business or continue in a career of self-employment
- Assistance may include support for:
  - Training in business start-up and development from an accredited business school;
  - Business Plan development;
  - Professional advice for marketing, accounting and financial management, and office location (e.g., incubators)
- * Incubators are designed to accelerate the development of a small start-up by partnering within a bigger business, thereby reducing start-up costs, access to experts and lateral contributions such as office supplies, furniture, etc.

## Slide 22: Group Trainingor Special Project
- Group Trainingor Special Project
- Funding is available for regionally-based community projects which support employment training and work experience
- Eligible projects must provide a combination of certification, life skills, pre-employment skills or practical work experience.
- Non-Profit organizations and private corporations must demonstrate capacity to provide the required management, supervision, coordination, financial report and necessary liability insurance for the project.
- Funding up to a maximum of 52 weeks

## Slide 62: LivingAllowance
- LivingAllowance
- There is NO minimum (or flat rate) amount for a living allowance.
  - Each client application MUST be assessed on its own merits. Providing ‘minimum’ amounts to clients who may not be eligible can put them at risk of having to repay those funds.
- A living allowance MAY be provided on a ‘needs basis’ to a client participating in a training/educational intervention.
- A living allowance is generally intended to help cover basic needs DURING (not before) the intervention starts and while the client is attending a training/educational institution.
- A living allowance is a subsidy and is NOT intended to cover all a client’s monthly expenses or a deficit.
- Back-dating a living allowance payment is NOT permissible under any circumstances.

## Slide 63: LivingAllowance Eligibility
- LivingAllowance Eligibility
- Clients MUST disclose ALL household income/revenue from all sources.
- The ‘Financial Overview’ MUST be completed and signed by the Client.
- Income Verification IS REQUIRED prior to any approval of funding.
- Coordinator MUST request documentation to verify monthly income and  expenses prior to recommending a living allowance.
- A Client on an active EI claim is NOT eligible for a living allowance until their claim is completed.
- A living allowance will NOT be paid until the ‘Client Monthly Attendance Report’ has been completed and signed by the Client and verified by the Coordinator.

## Slide 64: Living Allowance:Eligible Expenses
- Living Allowance:Eligible Expenses
- If eligible, a living allowance is intended to subsidize:
  - Rent/mortgage payment;
  - Monthly basic utilities (gas, water, hydro, sewer);
  - Groceries;
  - Transportation (bus/train pass)* - refer to previous slide
  - Childcare**
- *A Transportation subsidy is not intended to cover car payments, insurance or vehicle maintenance.
- **Childcare is only eligible if their current monthly expense increases as a direct result of the client’s training or educational program. Eligibility will be determined on a case-by-case basis – refer to previous slide.
- PLEASE NOTE:
- A living allowance is not intended to cover a client’s previous debts (i.e., credit cards, student loans, bank loans, etc.).
- Other expenses not eligible include such items as security systems, pet care, cellphones, entertainment, subscriptions, annual fees for certifications or licenses (outside of an active intervention), etc. (this is not a complete list of ineligible expenses).

## Slide 65: Living Allowance:Attendance Report
- Living Allowance:Attendance Report
- A living allowance is NOT to be processed for payment until the ‘Client Monthly Attendance Report’ has been completed and signed by the Client and verified by the Coordinator.
- Coordinator MUST advise clients that any false or misleading statements on their Attendance Report will be grounds for immediate suspension of their living allowance and may result in a repayment of funds for monies received to which they were not entitled.
- Clients MUST report all absences and, in some cases may be required to provide medical documentation to support absences from school, training or paid work placement.
- Each month, the Coordinator MUST attach the client’s attendance report to the EFT form for which the monthly allowance is to be paid and submit for payment.

## Slide 88: Authorization to Quit
- Authorization to Quit
- Section 25 Authority
- While in receipt of EI Part I benefits, clients must be available, capable, and actively seeking employment. If a client is attending a training or employment activity which they have initiated on their own, they may be deemed “unavailable” and therefore would not receive EI Part I benefits.
- Section 25 Authority (S25A) authorizes the ISPs to refer active EI Part I claimants to attend approved courses or programs of instruction. ISPs may also recommend that they participate in any other employment activity (interventions) designed to facilitate their re-entry into the labour force while maintaining entitlement to Part I benefits (staying on their active EI Part I benefit claim).
- S25A also allows the ISP to counsel a client to leave their employment (Authorization to Quit) to take part in an approved employment or training intervention if:
- a client has received a notice of imminent lay-off
- a client must leave their current job for medical reasons, and they face an employment barrier to obtaining new employment
- Typically, the Authorization to Quit (ATQ) or leave employment is only done while in an active Case Management situation, and typically within 2 weeks prior to the start date of the intervention. (The ATQ is not retroactive).
- Take Note: The Authorization to Quit is used only in extenuating circumstances!

## Slide 89: Authorization to Quit
- Authorization to Quit
- Authorizing a Client to Quit and the Training Referral
- An action plan is developed once a preliminary needs assessment is done with a client (based on information about the client’s work history and employment earnings), and the client signs the consent form and enters into case management. This action plan outlines the employment and training interventions that the client intends to participate in. Then the ISP can initiate the process to authorize the client to quit their employment to attend an ISET Program.
- The ISP would complete and sign an Authorization to Quit (in writing, using the ATQ form and providing an explanation) to support that the individual has been approved (referred) to attend the ISET training or employment intervention. A copy of the ATQ form must be provided to the client and the EI processing centre (through their regional process).
- The client will then apply for EI Part I and submit a copy of the ATQ along with their Records of Employment.
- ESDC will review an application for EI Part I benefits and will approve if an individual meets all qualifying requirements and entitlement conditions.
- The ATQ input cannot be done on the EIBIS system. However, by doing a query with the client’s SIN in EIBIS, the system will send a response to the ISP to advise if/when the client’s EI Part I Claim is finalized. The Training Referral can then be entered into the EIBIS, if the client’s application is finalized and deemed eligible.
- ATQs involve a high level of responsibility. Ultimately, the authority or decision to allow an individual to quit their employment lies with the ISET ISP. EI does not make the determination whether the quit is “permitted”. However, it can refuse this authority if the requirements of S25 are not met (for example, the S25A isn’t identified on the ISPs delegation list, the ATQ was back-dated, etc.).
